Read this carefully — every family is different

These numbers are realistic estimates based on Anna's Costa Blanca South data and Spanish statistics. Your real spending depends on choices the calculator can't make for you. Map this to your life:

↓ Where you can save
  • Skip school meals — pick the child up at 14:00 instead of 16:00 (~€1,500/yr saved per child)
  • Skip pet insurance — pay vet out-of-pocket (~€240/yr saved per pet, but a single emergency wipes it)
  • Cook at home — every €15 menú del día replaced by home cooking saves ~€350/mo for two
  • No car — coastal towns work on e-bikes / scooters. Saves ~€3,500/yr vs ownership
  • Solar panels — many CB South villas have them. Summer electricity bill can drop to €0
  • Public Spanish lessons — Escuela Oficial de Idiomas: €70–€150/yr instead of €700+ private
↑ What's NOT counted here
  • Travel — flights home, holidays in Spain, weekend trips
  • Hobbies that add up: gym €35–€50/mo · zumba €5/class · mini-golf €13–€17 · golf membership from €1,000/yr
  • Spa, beauty, dental work — Spanish dental is cheaper than UK/US but not free
  • Expensive shopping habits — designer clothes, electronics, gadgets
  • Major home repairs — roof, boiler, A/C unit replacement (€2k–€10k events)
  • Income/wealth tax if you're tax-resident (6+ months/year on worldwide income)

        What the calculator can't see

        The mistakes that cost international buyers tens of thousands

        Cost-of-living numbers are predictable. The expensive surprises come from decisions made before you even move in.

        €20,000–€40,000 overpay

        Buying at Idealista asking prices

        Asking prices on the Costa Blanca run 8–15% above what comparable properties actually close at. International buyers without sold-comparable data pay asking and call it a "negotiation."

        Fix: buyer-side comparable check + a structured offer with conditions.
        €3,000–€15,000/yr surprise

        Community fees you didn't see

        Gated urbanisations with pool, security and gardens carry community fees of €80–€300+/month. Plus pending derramas (special assessments) for roof or façade works the seller didn't mention.

        Fix: request community minutes for the last 3 years before signing.
        €4,000–€12,000+ per child / yr

        Picking the school after the house

        International schools in Costa Blanca South: €400–€1,200/month (10-month year). Bilingual concertado: a fraction of that. Summer camps €400–€900/month — and August has almost none, so plan childcare separately.

        Fix: school first, then narrow the property search to its catchment.
        €2,000–€8,000 wasted

        Furnishing fully before knowing the climate

        July–August on the Costa Blanca is hot. Without cross-ventilation or proper A/C, summer electricity hits €200–€350/month. Buyers furnish first, retrofit cooling, then resell furniture that doesn't fit the layout.

        Fix: live one summer before committing to furniture and HVAC.
        Five-figure tax surprise

        Becoming tax resident without planning

        183+ days in Spain = Spanish tax resident on worldwide income and wealth. Wealth tax thresholds vary by region. Non-resident owners also file Modelo 210 every year — even on empty property.

        Fix: talk to a tax adviser before your arrival date.
        €2k–€5k recovered

        Negotiating the rental contract

        Standard Spanish rental contracts default to landlord-favourable terms: deposit conditions, exit penalties, maintenance liability, annual increases. Most international tenants sign without reading the fine print.

        Fix: independent representative reviews and negotiates before you sign.
        Renting from abroad

        You can sign a rental remotely — with the right local representative

        Plenty of international buyers successfully secure long-term rentals on the Costa Blanca before they land. It works when there's an independent representative on the ground — someone whose loyalty is to you, not the agency listing the property.

        The risk isn't renting remotely. The risk is renting remotely through the same agent or platform that's also representing the landlord.
